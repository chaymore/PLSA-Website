What is the LSAT?

If law school is on your radar, you’ve definitely crossed paths with the Law School Admission Test (LSAT). Think of it as the first major milestone of your legal career. Outside of a few schools accepting the GRE, practically every accredited US law school relies heavily on this score. Along with your undergraduate GPA, it is the primary factor admissions committees evaluate.

Why is it so important?

The LSAT remains the single most influential metric in law school admissions because it acts as a standardized equalizer. While GPAs vary based on institutional rigor and major difficulty, the LSAT gives admissions teams a uniform benchmark for critical reading and analytical deduction. Furthermore, because class medians drive law school rankings, a strong LSAT score is often the primary key to securing significant merit-based scholarships.
