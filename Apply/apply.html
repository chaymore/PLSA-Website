Applying to law school

Build a thoughtful application with expert BYU advising and a student community beside you.

Your first stop

Start with BYU’s pre-law advisor.

Application choices are personal: timing, school selection, written materials, recommendations, and finances all depend on your goals. For individualized guidance, PLSA recommends beginning with Kris Tina Carlston at BYU’s Preprofessional Advisement Center.

PLSA complements that professional advising with workshops, peer perspective, and a community of students working through the same process.

BYU pre-law advising

Kris Tina Carlston

Director, Preprofessional Advisement Center

Kris Tina advises BYU students interested in law and brings both legal and business training to the conversation. She earned her J.D. from BYU’s J. Reuben Clark Law School and an MBA from Utah State University’s Huntsman School of Business. Before coming to BYU, she practiced in Chicago in corporate tax, land exchanges, and nonprofit organizations; she also works in estate planning.

Meet with Kris Tina for individualized application guidance. BYU’s advising page asks students to attend an information workshop before scheduling a longer appointment; quick questions can also be handled during virtual drop-in hours.

More application support

You do not have to polish every piece alone.

The Preprofessional Advisement Center also offers application editing and interview preparation. Its editor can help with personal statements and application materials after the recommended workshop, while interview resources can help you practice before law-school conversations.
